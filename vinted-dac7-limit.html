Vinted DAC7 limit: 30 sales or €2,000 a year

Short answer: under the EU rule called DAC7, an online marketplace such as Vinted, eBay or Kleinanzeigen must report you to the tax authority if you make 30 or more sales or receive €2,000 or more on that platform in one calendar year. Either limit is enough. The UK has applied the same rules since 2024, with a money limit of about £1,700.

What DAC7 is

DAC7 is the EU's seventh Directive on Administrative Cooperation (Directive (EU) 2021/514). Since 2023, online platforms must collect seller details and send a yearly report to the tax authority, which shares it with the seller's home country. The UK introduced its own version in 2024.

What is reported

  • Your name, address and tax identification number or date of birth
  • The account or bank details used for payouts
  • The number of sales and the amount you received in each quarter of the year

Platforms collect these details from you, and in the UK they must give sellers a copy of what they send.

Reported does not mean taxed

The report only tells the tax authority what you sold. In most countries, selling your own used belongings for less than you paid is not taxable income. Buying things in order to resell them at a profit can count as a business and may be taxable. The rules depend on your country, so ask your tax office if you are unsure.

Each platform counts on its own

The limits apply per platform. If you sell 20 items on Vinted and 20 on eBay, neither platform reports you, because the platforms do not add your sales together. That is why Relisto counts each marketplace separately.

Questions

What is the DAC7 limit for Vinted sellers?

A platform must report you to the tax authority if, in one calendar year, you make 30 or more sales of goods or receive €2,000 or more (about £1,700 in the UK) on that platform. Reaching either limit is enough. Below both limits, nothing is reported.

Does DAC7 mean I have to pay tax?

No. Reporting is not taxing. In most countries selling your own used things for less than you paid is not taxable income, while buying things to resell for a profit can count as a business. The rules depend on your country.

Are the limits counted per platform?

Yes. Each platform counts on its own. 20 sales on one platform and 20 on another are not added together by the platforms.

When does the count start again?

On 1 January. The limits apply to each calendar year, and platforms send their report to the tax authority after the year ends.

What information is reported about me?

The platform reports your name, address, tax identification number or date of birth, the account and bank details used for payouts, the number of sales and the amounts you received.
