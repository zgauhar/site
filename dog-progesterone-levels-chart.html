Dog progesterone levels chart

Progesterone starts to rise just before ovulation. Breeders and vets use a series of blood tests to find the day she ovulates, which is the key date for timing the breeding and for the due date (63 days later).

1 ng/mL = 3.18 nmol/L. These are typical ranges. Labs and test methods (in-clinic analysers and reference labs) can read differently, so use your lab's reference ranges and stay with the same lab for the whole series.

How the ovulation day is estimated

  • The first test of 4 ng/mL or more is taken as the ovulation day.
  • If the tests have only reached the LH-surge range (1.5–3.9 ng/mL), ovulation is estimated as 2 days after the first test in that range. A test above 4 ng/mL confirms it.
  • Example: 0.9 ng/mL on 1 March, 2.1 on 3 March, 5.4 on 5 March. Ovulation is 5 March, the best days for a natural mating are 6 to 9 March, and the due date is 7 May.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start progesterone testing?

Around day 5 or 6 of the heat (counting the first day of bleeding as day 1), then every 2–3 days. Once the result starts to rise, test daily or as your vet advises.

What progesterone level means ovulation in dogs?

Ovulation usually happens at about 4–8 ng/mL (roughly 12.7–25 nmol/L). The LH surge comes first, at about 1.5–3.9 ng/mL, about two days before ovulation.

How do I convert ng/mL to nmol/L?

Multiply by 3.18. For example, 5 ng/mL is 15.9 nmol/L. To go back, divide nmol/L by 3.18. The calculator does it for you when you pick the unit.

Can I rely on the calculator instead of my vet?

No. It shows the standard interpretation of a test series. Results depend on the lab and the dog, and your vet or reproduction specialist decides when to breed.
