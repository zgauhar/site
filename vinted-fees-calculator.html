Vinted fees calculator

Short answer: private sellers pay no selling fee on Vinted. The buyer pays a Vinted fee (formerly the Buyer Protection fee) at checkout, which Vinted describes as usually 5% of the item price + €0.70 in France, Germany, Italy and Spain, 5% + 2.90 zł in Poland and 3% to 8% + £0.30 to £0.80 in the UK. A €20.00 item therefore costs the buyer about €21.70 before postage, and you receive the full €20.00.

Vinted says the fee is “usually” this and can depend on the item, the order value and whether it is a bundle. The exact amount is shown on every listing.

How the Vinted fee is calculated

The fee is a percentage of the item or bundle price plus a fixed amount, and it includes VAT. It is added at checkout and shown in the price breakdown of every listing, so buyers see it before they pay. Postage is paid on top and is not part of the percentage.

What it means for your prices

  • Small items carry the biggest share: on a €5.00 item the fee is €0.95, about 19% of the price. Bundling cheap items lowers the share for the buyer.
  • Price for the buyer’s total: buyers compare the total, not your price. Use the budget field to find the highest price that keeps the total under a round number.
  • Your real profit is the sale price minus what you paid and any postage or packaging you cover. Work it out with the resale profit calculator.

Questions

Do sellers pay fees on Vinted?

Private sellers pay no selling fee. The buyer pays the Vinted fee at checkout. Optional extras such as paid promotion are separate.

How much is the Vinted fee?

Vinted describes it as usually 5% of the price + €0.70 in France, Germany, Italy and Spain, 5% + 2.90 zł in Poland and 3% to 8% + £0.30 to £0.80 in the UK. The exact fee is shown on each listing.

Is the Vinted fee charged on postage?

No. The percentage is calculated on the item or bundle price. Postage is added separately.

Is the Buyer Protection fee the same as the Vinted fee?

Yes. Vinted’s help centre now calls the buyer-side fee the “Vinted fee”. It used to be called the Buyer Protection fee.
