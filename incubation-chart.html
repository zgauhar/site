Day 0 is the day the eggs go in. Lockdown is the day you stop turning and raise the humidity. Temperatures are for forced-air (fan) incubators; still-air incubators need about 1 °C (2 °F) more, measured at the top of the eggs. Humidity is relative humidity. Values are the consensus of incubator makers' manuals and university poultry extension guides; always follow your own incubator's manual.

Rules that apply to every species

Turning

Turn at least 3 times a day, always an odd number, until lockdown. An odd number means the egg spends alternate nights on alternate sides. Automatic turners do this for you. Stop at lockdown.

Temperature

Temperature matters more than anything else. Check it with a second, calibrated thermometer at egg height. A steady half degree too warm or too cool shifts the hatch by about a day and lowers the hatch rate.

Humidity

Eggs must lose about 13 % of their weight before they hatch. Use the chart values as a starting point and adjust by watching the air cell. See the humidity guide.

Lockdown and hatch

From lockdown on, keep the lid shut. Opening it drops the humidity and can glue pipped chicks to the shell. Leave the chicks in until they are dry and fluffy.

Waterfowl: cooling and misting

Duck and goose eggs have a thicker shell and more fat in the yolk. Many keepers cool them once a day from day 10 until lockdown: open the incubator for 10–15 minutes and mist the eggs with lukewarm water. It helps the eggs lose moisture and mimics the mother leaving the nest to feed.

Before you set the eggs

  • Run the incubator for 24 hours first and check it holds a steady temperature.
  • Let shipped eggs rest pointy end down for 12–24 hours at room temperature.
  • Set eggs that are less than 7–10 days old; hatch rates fall with every extra day of storage.
  • Don't wash hatching eggs. Set clean eggs only, and wash your hands before handling them.
