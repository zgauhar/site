When to wean baby rabbits: 4 to 8 weeks, how to do it and separating bucks from does

Baby rabbits are usually weaned between 4 and 8 weeks old: around 6 weeks in many meat rabbitries and around 8 weeks for show and pet rabbits. By then they eat hay and pellets on their own and drink from the water bottle or bowl. Wean calmly, keep the litter together and the feed the same, then sex and separate bucks from does by about 10 to 12 weeks.

Weaning age at a glance

AgeWhat it usually means
Under 4 weeksToo young. Kits still rely on the doe's milk.
4 to 5 weeksThe earliest weaning. Needs extra care and steady feed.
6 weeksCommon in meat rabbitries.
8 weeksCommon for show and pet rabbits; kits are bigger and sturdier.
10 to 12 weeksSex and separate bucks from does if not done at weaning.

Signs the kits are ready

  • They eat hay and pellets on their own every day, not just nibble.
  • They drink from the bottle or bowl without help.
  • They are active, bright and growing steadily.
  • The doe spends more time away from them and nurses less.

If a litter is small, slow-growing or the weather is very hot or cold, many breeders wait an extra week.

How to wean without stress

  1. Take the doe out, not the kits. Kits that stay in the cage they know have one change to deal with instead of two.
  2. Keep the litter together for the first week or two after weaning.
  3. Don't change the feed. Same pellets, same hay, plenty of fresh water. Sudden feed changes around weaning are a common cause of tummy trouble in young rabbits.
  4. Stagger it if you like. Some breeders wean the biggest kits first and leave the smallest with the doe for a few more days.
  5. Watch droppings and appetite every day for the next two weeks. Soft droppings, a dirty bottom, a bloated belly or a kit that stops eating needs a vet quickly: young rabbits go downhill fast.

Sexing and separating bucks and does

Young rabbits mature fast. Bucks can start trying to breed from about 3 to 4 months, earlier in some small breeds, and they start to fight as they get older. Sex the kits at weaning or soon after, recheck a week or two later (young rabbits are easy to mis-sex), and move the bucks to their own space by about 10 to 12 weeks. Tattoo or mark the keepers so each one's record follows it.

What about the doe?

Give her a little time to dry off once the kits are gone, and keep an eye on her teats for heat, hardness or swelling over the next days. When to rebreed her depends on her condition, age and your breeding plan. Note her litter results first: kits born alive, kits weaned and the day-21 litter weight show how well she did as a mother.

Frequently asked questions

What age should baby rabbits be weaned?

Between 4 and 8 weeks. Kits are rarely weaned before 4 weeks; 6 weeks is common for meat rabbits and 8 weeks for show and pet rabbits, which gives them more time on the doe's milk.

Should I take the doe away or the kits?

Many breeders take the doe out and leave the kits in the cage they know, which keeps the change smaller. Others move the kits as a group. Either way, keep the litter together for a while after weaning.

When should baby rabbits be separated by sex?

Around 10 to 12 weeks for most breeds, earlier for small breeds that mature quickly. Young bucks can start trying to breed from about 3 to 4 months, and they also start to fight, so sex and separate them before then.

What do baby rabbits eat after weaning?

The same hay and pellets they were already eating with the doe, with fresh water always available. Keep the feed unchanged for the first couple of weeks after weaning and introduce new foods slowly, one at a time.
