What size cable for an induction hob or electric cooker?

A full-size induction hob is rated about 7.4 kW, which is 32 A at 230 V, so it needs its own 32 A circuit. Clipped to a wall on a short run that circuit needs 4 mm² copper cable. In an insulated wall it needs 6 mm², and beyond about 30 m voltage drop pushes it to 10 mm². Where the hob is wired to two or three phases, 2.5 mm² on 16 A breakers is common instead.

Hob and cooker current: kW to amps

Induction hobs and ovens are rated in kilowatts. At 230 V single-phase the current is the power divided by the voltage:

Plug-in induction hob (2 zones)2.8 to 3.7 kW12 to 16 A
Built-in oven2.5 to 3.6 kW11 to 16 A
4-zone induction hob7.2 to 7.4 kW31 to 32 A
5-zone or wide induction hob10 to 11 kW43 to 48 A (normally three-phase)
Free-standing electric range9 to 12 kW39 to 52 A before diversity

The rating plate or installation manual gives the exact figure. Many hobs also have a power-limit setting that caps them at, for example, 16 or 20 A, which lets them run on a smaller circuit.

Cable size for a 32 A hob by length and installation method

Copper PVC cable at 30 °C, two loaded conductors, not grouped with other circuits, sized for a 3% voltage drop (6.9 V at 230 V). The cable must carry the 32 A breaker rating after derating.

Installation10 m20 m30 m40 m
C, clipped direct to a wall4 mm²6 mm²10 mm²10 mm²
A1, in an insulated wall6 mm²6 mm²10 mm²10 mm²

The capacities come from IEC 60364-5-52 Annex B: 4 mm² carries 36 A clipped direct but only 26 A in an insulated wall, so it fails there; 6 mm² carries 46 A and 34 A. Voltage drop at 32 A is about 0.35 V per metre on 4 mm², 0.23 V on 6 mm² and 0.14 V on 10 mm², which gives limits of roughly 19 m, 29 m and 49 m.

Hob and oven on one circuit: cooker diversity

A hob almost never runs every zone at full power for long, and the oven thermostat cycles. Wiring rules allow for this with a diversity allowance. The UK method (IET On-Site Guide) for a household cooker circuit is:

  1. Take the first 10 A of the combined rated current in full.
  2. Add 30% of the remaining current.
  3. Add 5 A if the cooker control unit includes a socket outlet.
Appliances on the circuitTotal ratedAfter diversityUsual circuit

7.4 kW hob + 3.6 kW oven + socket48 A26.4 A32 A, 6 mm²
11 kW range + socket48 A26.4 A32 A, 6 mm²

Diversity lowers the design current, not the breaker rule: the breaker must still be at least the design current and the cable must still carry the breaker rating. Check that both appliance manuals allow a shared supply, and check your national rules, because not every country uses the same allowance.

Two-phase and three-phase hob connections

In much of continental Europe the hob is wired to a 400 V supply with a 5-core cable, commonly 5 × 2.5 mm² on three 16 A breakers. The hob's terminal block splits the zones across two or three phases, so each phase carries 16 A or less. If you move a hob between countries, or between single-phase and three-phase homes, change the links on the terminal block as the manual shows. A wrong link can put 400 V across a 230 V element.

Checklist before you install or swap a hob

  • Read the rating plate. A replacement hob is often more powerful than the old one, so the old cable may not pass.
  • Dedicated circuit. A full-size hob or cooker has its own circuit and a double-pole isolator within reach, not behind the appliance.
  • Breaker rule. IB ≤ In ≤ Iz: design current, breaker, cable capacity. See how to choose a breaker size for a cable.
  • Route. Cable under kitchen insulation or bunched with other circuits needs derating. Use the insulated-wall row if in doubt.
  • Long runs. Kitchens far from the consumer unit hit the voltage-drop limit first. The maths is in why a cable that carries the current can still be too long.
  • Total load. A hob, an electric shower and an EV charger together can exceed a small main fuse.
  • Local rules. National wiring rules can be stricter, and the installer remains responsible for the design.

Frequently asked questions

What size cable for a 7.4 kW induction hob?

A 7.4 kW hob draws about 32 A at 230 V and goes on a 32 A breaker. Clipped direct to a wall (method C) 4 mm² copper is enough up to about 19 m for a 3% voltage drop; in an insulated wall (method A1) use 6 mm², which reaches about 29 m. Longer runs need 10 mm².

Can an induction hob and an oven share one cooker circuit?

Often yes, if the manufacturers allow it. In the UK the cooker circuit is sized with diversity: the first 10 A, plus 30% of the rest, plus 5 A if the cooker control unit has a socket. A 7.4 kW hob and a 3 kW oven come to about 26 A, which fits a 32 A circuit.

Why is my induction hob on 16 A breakers?

In much of continental Europe hobs are connected to two or three phases of a 400 V supply, usually with 5-core 2.5 mm² cable on 16 A breakers. The hob splits its zones across the phases, so no single phase carries the full 32 A.

Can I plug an induction hob into a normal socket?

Only small hobs sold with a plug, which limit their total power to about 3 to 3.7 kW. A full-size hob of 7 kW or more needs its own hard-wired circuit; never fit a plug to a hob that was not supplied with one.
