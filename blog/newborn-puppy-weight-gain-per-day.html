How much weight should a newborn puppy gain per day?

A healthy newborn puppy gains about 5 to 10% of its body weight every day and doubles its birth weight by day 7 to 10. Weigh each puppy at birth and then daily on a gram scale. A puppy that stops gaining, keeps losing after the first 48 hours, or has not doubled by day 10 needs a closer look and usually a call to your vet.

Why daily weights matter

A newborn puppy cannot tell you it is not getting enough milk, and the first signs of trouble are easy to miss in a busy whelping box. The scale picks it up a day or two before the puppy looks weak. That is why breeders weigh every puppy daily: a flat or falling weight is the earliest warning of poor suckling, low milk supply, cold, or illness, often linked to what is called fading puppy syndrome.

Newborn puppy weight gain targets

AgeWhat to expect
Day 0Birth weight: the baseline for everything that follows
Day 1 to 2Small loss is common, up to about 10% of birth weight
Day 3 onwardsGaining every day, about 5 to 10% of current body weight
Day 7 to 10Birth weight doubled
Week 2 to 3Still a steady daily gain; eyes open, more active feeding

As an example, a 300 g puppy should be gaining roughly 15 to 30 g a day and weigh about 600 g by day 7 to 10. Toy-breed puppies of 100 g gain only 5 to 10 g a day, so a single gram matters, while giant-breed puppies may put on 50 g or more. That is why breeders track the percentage, not just the grams. How to weigh newborn puppies

  1. Use a digital scale that reads in 1 g steps. A kitchen or postal scale is fine. Put a small bowl or box with a towel on it and tare it to zero.
  2. Weigh at birth, once the puppy is dry and has fed, and write it down straight away.
  3. Weigh at the same time every day. Twice a day for the first three or four days is better, because that is when problems develop fastest.
  4. Tell the puppies apart. Use coloured collars or ribbons so each weight goes to the right puppy.
  5. Compare with yesterday, not with littermates. A small puppy that gains steadily is doing better than a big one that has stalled.

Warning signs: when a puppy is not gaining weight

  • Still losing weight after 48 hours, or a loss of more than 10% of birth weight.
  • No gain for a full day after the first two days.
  • Not doubled by day 10.
  • Weak suckling, crying a lot, feeling cool to the touch, or lying apart from the litter.

Check that the puppy can latch, gets time on the fuller back teats and is kept warm, then call your vet. Supplementary bottle or tube feeding helps, but get shown how first, as tube feeding done wrong is dangerous. This guide gives general information, not veterinary advice.

Frequently asked questions

How much weight should a newborn puppy gain per day?

About 5 to 10% of its current body weight a day in the first two weeks. A 300 g puppy should gain roughly 15 to 30 g a day. The simplest check is that it doubles its birth weight by day 7 to 10.

Is it normal for a newborn puppy to lose weight?

A small loss in the first 24 to 48 hours is common, up to about 10% of the birth weight. After that the weight should go up every day. A puppy that is still losing after 48 hours, or loses more than 10%, needs attention from you and usually your vet.

How often should I weigh newborn puppies?

At birth, then at the same time every day for at least the first two weeks. Many breeders weigh twice a day for the first three to four days, when problems develop fastest. From week three, every two to three days is usually enough.

When should a puppy double its birth weight?

By day 7 to 10. Many healthy puppies do it close to day 7. If a puppy has not doubled its birth weight by day 10, talk to your vet.

What scale should I use to weigh puppies?

A digital kitchen or postal scale that reads in 1 g steps, with a small bowl or box on top that you tare to zero. Use the same scale every time so the readings are comparable.
