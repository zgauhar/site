How to budget on an irregular income: a 6-step plan for freelance, gig and tip earners

To budget on an irregular income, plan around the lowest income you can count on, not your average, then pay yourself a steady amount from a holding account and decide in advance where any extra money goes. Good months fill the buffer, lean months draw on it, and your day-to-day spending stays the same. It works for freelancers, gig workers, commission sales, seasonal work and tipped jobs.

Why normal budgets break with irregular pay

Most budgeting advice assumes the same salary lands on the same day. With variable pay, a budget built on an average month fails twice: in a good month the extra quietly disappears, and in a bad month the bills arrive anyway. The fix is to separate when money arrives from when you spend it.

The 6-step irregular income budget

  1. Find your floor. Look at the last 12 months of income after tax. Take the average of your three lowest months. That is your baseline, the amount you plan your life on.
  2. Cover essentials from the floor. Rent, utilities, insurance, transport, food and minimum debt payments must fit inside the baseline. If they don't, that is the number to work on first.
  3. Open a holding account. Every payment goes there first, never straight into your spending account.
  4. Pay yourself a steady amount. Once a month (or every two weeks) move your baseline from the holding account to your spending account, like a salary.
  5. Set aside tax from every payment. If tax is not taken from your pay, move a fixed share to a separate account the day money arrives. Many freelancers use 20 to 30 percent as a rule of thumb; check your country's rates.
  6. Give extra money a job. Anything above the baseline is not spending money until it has been assigned, in a fixed order you decide in advance (see below).

Worked example

Say your income after tax over the last year ranged from 1,400 to 3,100 a month, and your three lowest months were 1,400, 1,550 and 1,600.

StepAmount
Average of the three lowest months1,517, round down to 1,500
Essential bills each month950
Left for day-to-day spending550
Daily budget over a 30-day monthabout 18 a day

In a 2,600 month you still pay yourself 1,500. The extra 1,100 stays in the holding account and gets its job. In a 1,300 month, the missing 200 comes out of the buffer, and your daily number does not change.

Where extra money goes, in order

  1. Tax, if you haven't already set it aside.
  2. Buffer until the holding account has one month of essentials in it. Then you live on last month's income, which is the single biggest stress reducer.
  3. Sinking funds for costs you know are coming, such as car repairs, insurance renewals and holidays. See our guide to sinking funds for irregular expenses.
  4. Emergency fund and debt. Grow a separate emergency fund of a few months of essentials, and pay down high-interest debt.
  5. Fun money. Set a share of every good month aside to enjoy, so the plan is easy to stick to.

Tips, gigs and cash days

If you earn in small amounts most days, a monthly plan is too slow. Set your daily budget from the baseline, then add tips or an extra gig as one-off income on the day it comes in. Your allowance for the rest of the period rises a little, and a slow week does not break the plan. If you are paid weekly or every two weeks on top of variable extras, our guide to budgeting on weekly, biweekly or twice-a-month pay shows how to line bills up with paydays.

Common mistakes

  • Budgeting on your best month. One great month is not a raise. Plan on the floor.
  • Spending straight from the account money lands in. Without a holding account, every payment feels like a pay rise.
  • Forgetting tax. A tax bill at the end of the year is the most common way a good freelance year turns into debt.
  • Never adjusting. Recalculate your baseline every six months or when work changes.

Frequently asked questions

How do you budget when your income changes every month?

Budget on a low, reliable amount instead of an average: for example the average of your three lowest months in the past year. Cover essentials from that amount, pay yourself the same sum each month from a holding account, and decide in advance where any extra goes.

How much buffer do I need with an irregular income?

A useful first target is one month of essential costs in the holding account, so you live on last month's income instead of this month's. After that, build a separate emergency fund of a few months of essentials.

How much should freelancers set aside for tax?

It depends on your country and income. A common rule of thumb is 20 to 30 percent of each payment, moved to a separate account the day it arrives. Check your local rates or ask an accountant for your own number.

Can a daily budget work with tips or gig income?

Yes. Set your daily budget from your reliable baseline, then add tips or extra gigs as one-off income on the day they arrive. The extra raises your allowance for the rest of the period without changing your plan.
