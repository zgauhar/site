When to put a nest box in for a pregnant rabbit, and how to set it up

Put the nest box in on day 27 to 29 after the breeding, a few days before the kits are due on about day 31. Too early and many does treat it as a toilet or a bed and the bedding is soiled by kindling day; too late and she may kindle on the cage floor, where newborn kits chill and die quickly. Fill it with clean, dry bedding and let her build the nest herself.

Why day 27 to 29?

Rabbits are pregnant for about 31 days, and most does kindle on day 31 or 32, with day 28 to 35 still normal. Putting the box in three or four days ahead gives her time to find it, carry hay around and pull fur, without days of sitting in it beforehand. Nest box size

The box should let the doe get in, turn and lie down, but stay small enough that the kits stay together in one warm pile. Typical sizes breeders use:

  • Small breeds (Netherland Dwarf, Holland Lop, Mini Rex): about 25 × 40 cm (10 × 16 in).
  • Medium breeds (New Zealand, Californian, Rex): about 30 × 45 cm (12 × 18 in).
  • Giant breeds (Flemish Giant): larger, about 35 × 60 cm (14 × 24 in).

A lip or partial lid at the front helps keep the kits inside in the first days. Wood, or metal with a removable floor, both work; whatever you use must be easy to clean and dry out between litters.

How to set it up

  1. Start clean and dry. Scrub and fully dry the box after every litter.
  2. Floor layer: a few centimetres of pine shavings or a similar absorbent material. Avoid cedar shavings, whose oils can irritate.
  3. Nesting material on top: fill the box generously with clean hay or straw. The doe hollows it out and shapes the nest herself.
  4. Put it where she wants it. Many does pick a dark corner away from the feeder. If she keeps building somewhere else, move the box there.
  5. Let her pull the fur. Most does line the nest with fur from their chest and belly shortly before or just after kindling. Keep a bag of clean fur from earlier litters in case a first-time doe pulls too little.

Cold weather and hot weather

  • Cold: fill the box deeper with straw, block draughts, and consider a box with a solid lid. Kits can't keep themselves warm for the first week or so.
  • Hot: use less bedding, leave the top open, and make sure the kits can spread out. Kits can overheat under a thick pile of fur in summer, so remove some fur if they look damp and restless.

When to take the nest box out

Take it out at about 3 weeks after kindling, once the kits are hopping in and out on their own and starting to nibble hay and pellets. In cold weather leave it in a week or so longer; in hot weather, or once it is wet and dirty, take it out a little earlier. The free baby rabbit age calculator gives the date from the kindling day. Frequently asked questions

When should I put the nest box in for my rabbit?

Around day 27 to 29 after the breeding, a few days before the kits are due on about day 31. Earlier and many does use it as a toilet or sleep in it; later and she may kindle before it is in.

What bedding goes in a rabbit nest box?

A layer of absorbent material such as pine shavings on the floor, then plenty of clean, dry hay or straw for the doe to shape into a nest. She lines it with fur she pulls herself. Avoid cedar shavings and anything damp, dusty or mouldy.

How big should a rabbit nest box be?

Big enough for the doe to get in and turn around, but small enough that the kits stay together in one warm pile. As a rough guide, about 25 by 40 cm for small breeds, 30 by 45 cm for medium breeds such as New Zealands, and larger for giants.

When do you take the nest box out?

At about 3 weeks after kindling, once the kits are hopping in and out on their own. Leave it in longer in cold weather, and take it out earlier if it gets wet and dirty in hot weather.
