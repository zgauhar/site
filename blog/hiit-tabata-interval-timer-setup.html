HIIT and Tabata timer: how to set the work and rest intervals

A Tabata timer is set to 20 seconds of work and 10 seconds of rest, repeated for 8 rounds, which is exactly 4 minutes. Other HIIT workouts use longer blocks, such as 30 seconds on and 30 off, or 40 on and 20 off. What matters is picking a work-to-rest ratio you can sustain with good form to the very last round, then letting an interval timer run it so you never watch the clock.

What is an interval timer?

An interval timer chains short steps, usually work and rest, and repeats them for a set number of rounds. It rings at every change, so you know when to start and stop without looking. It is the same idea as a Pomodoro timer, only with seconds instead of minutes.

Add a 5-minute warm-up and a few minutes of cool-down around any of these. Tabata is brutally hard when done properly, so most people do one or two blocks with a few minutes of easy movement between them.

How to choose the ratio

  • New to intervals: start at 1:2 so your form holds up. You can shorten the rest later.
  • Regular exerciser: 1:1 is the usual middle ground, and it is easy to remember.
  • Short, intense moves such as sprints, burpees or kettlebell swings suit 20 s work; steadier moves such as squats and lunges suit 40 s.
  • Judge it by the last round. If your reps fall apart in round 7, lengthen the rest. Do not just slow down.

How to set up the timer

  1. Create one work step and one rest step, each with its own sound or label so you can tell them apart without looking.
  2. Set the number of rounds. For Tabata it is 8.
  3. Add a short get-ready countdown of 10 seconds so you can reach position.
  4. Test it once with the volume where you will really train. The alarm must be audible over music and breathing.

Mistakes that ruin an interval session

  1. Starting too hard. Going all out in round 1 leaves nothing for round 8.
  2. Silent or similar alarms. If work and rest sound the same, you will glance at the screen every few seconds.
  3. Cutting the rest. Rest is what lets the next round be hard. Shortening it turns HIIT into a slow slog.
  4. Phone locks and the timer stops. Use a timer with exact alarms that keep running when the screen is off.
  5. Too many hard sessions. Two or three HIIT sessions a week with easier days between is plenty for most people.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Tabata timer setting?

20 seconds of all-out work followed by 10 seconds of rest, repeated for 8 rounds. The whole session is 4 minutes, plus a warm-up and cool-down.

How long should work and rest intervals be for HIIT?

Common ratios are 1:2 for beginners (for example 20 s work, 40 s rest), 1:1 for most people (30 s on, 30 s off) and 2:1 (Tabata is 20 s on, 10 s off) for fit users. Pick the ratio where you can still keep good form in the last round.

How many rounds of HIIT should I do?

Around 6 to 10 rounds per block, for 10 to 20 minutes of work. If you cannot keep the effort up in the last rounds, cut a round or lengthen the rest rather than slowing the work.
