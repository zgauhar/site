How to tell if a rabbit is pregnant: palpation from day 10 to 14, and the signs after

The reliable way to tell if a rabbit is pregnant is to palpate her 10 to 14 days after the breeding: the growing kits feel like small marbles in a row low in her belly. Most other signs, such as a fuller belly and nest building, only show in the last week of the 31-day pregnancy, and a false pregnancy can copy them. Palpating early means an empty doe can be rebred straight away instead of waiting a month.

When to palpate

Days after the breedingWhat you can feel
Before day 10Usually nothing: the kits are too small.
Day 10 to 14The best window. Separate, firm lumps about the size of a marble, in a row.
Day 15 to 18Larger, softer swellings that run together; harder to read and easier to confuse with the gut.
Last weekA full, firm belly. Kits may be felt moving, but by now nest building usually tells you anyway.

Many breeders palpate on day 12. The rabbit gestation calculator gives you the palpation day, the nest box day and the kindling date from the breeding date.

How to palpate a doe, step by step

  1. Set her on a non-slip surface, such as a towel or carpet square on a table, facing you, so she feels secure.
  2. Keep her still and calm with one hand resting over her shoulders. Never lift or hold a rabbit by the ears.
  3. Slide the other hand under her belly, palm up, just in front of her hind legs, with your thumb on one side and your fingers on the other.
  4. Move gently forward and back along the lower belly, letting the contents slide between your thumb and fingers. Don't squeeze or press up hard.
  5. Feel for a row of firm, round lumps of similar size, on one or both sides, low down and near the pelvis.

Take it slowly and stop if she struggles. The first few times, it helps to learn from an experienced breeder or your vet, who can show you what an empty doe and a pregnant doe feel like.

Kits or droppings?

  • Kits sit low in the belly, close to the pelvis, are about the same size and stay in place in a row.
  • Droppings are smaller, scattered higher up in the gut, and move along when you feel them.
  • Kidneys are higher, close to the spine; don't mistake them for kits.

If you aren't sure, write "unsure" and palpate again two or three days later rather than rebreeding.

Pregnant or false pregnancy?

A doe can ovulate without conceiving, after an infertile mating or even after being mounted by another doe. She then acts pregnant for about 16 to 18 days, and may pull fur and build a nest around that time with no kits on the way. A doe that palpated empty at day 12 and builds a nest at day 17 is almost certainly in a false pregnancy. Most can be rebred once it ends.

Signs later in the pregnancy

  • Weight gain: weighing her once a week shows a steady rise in the last two weeks.
  • A fuller, rounder belly in the final week.
  • Nest building: carrying hay around, usually from about day 25 to 28.
  • Fur pulling: she lines the nest with fur from her chest and belly, often just hours before kindling. Some does, especially first-timers, only pull fur afterwards.

Some breeders also "test breed" by putting the doe back with the buck; a pregnant doe often refuses him. It is unreliable, though: plenty of empty does refuse and some pregnant ones accept. When she does kindle, our guide to the first 24 hours after kindling covers what to check.

Frequently asked questions

How soon can you tell if a rabbit is pregnant?

By palpation, from about 10 to 14 days after the breeding. Before day 10 the kits are too small to feel, and after about day 16 they are harder to tell apart from the gut. Visible signs such as a fuller belly and nest building usually come only in the last week.

What do kits feel like when you palpate a doe?

Small, firm, marble-sized lumps in a row low in the belly, just in front of the pelvis. Droppings feel similar but are smaller, scattered and higher up in the gut, and they move.

Can a rabbit have a false pregnancy?

Yes. After an infertile mating, or even being mounted by another doe, she can act pregnant for about 16 to 18 days and may pull fur and build a nest around then. Palpation at day 10 to 14 tells the two apart.

Is palpating a rabbit safe?

When done gently, it is routine practice for breeders. Never squeeze or press hard, keep the doe calm and supported, and learn it from an experienced breeder or your vet before trying alone.
