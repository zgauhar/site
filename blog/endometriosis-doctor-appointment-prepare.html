How to prepare for an endometriosis doctor appointment

To prepare for an endometriosis appointment, bring a one-page summary of the last two to three months: your pain scores, where and when it hurts, your period dates, what you have tried and what it stopped you doing, plus a short list of questions. Appointments are brief and pain is hard to describe on demand, so records you made at the time are more reliable than memory and let the doctor see the pattern quickly.

This article is general information, not medical advice. A symptom diary helps you describe what you experience; it cannot diagnose anything. Talk to your doctor about your symptoms and the right care for you.

Why preparation changes the appointment

You may have ten to twenty minutes, and the questions you are asked, such as "how bad is the pain?", are exactly the ones that are hard to answer from memory. A good day on the day of the appointment can make you play it down. Notes taken as it happened keep the picture honest and save time for the part that matters: deciding what to do next.

Turn the diary into a short summary

A doctor cannot read ninety daily entries in a few minutes, so reduce them to a few lines you can say or hand over:

  • How many days in the last month had pain, and how many were 7 out of 10 or worse.
  • Which day of your cycle the worst pain tends to fall on.
  • Which types of pain are most frequent and which are the most severe.
  • The effect on work, sleep and normal life.
  • What changed after any treatment, and when.

Five questions worth asking

  1. What do you think is going on, and how sure are you?
  2. What are the options, and what are the trade-offs of each?
  3. Is a scan or another test useful for me, and what would it change?
  4. How long should I try this before we review it, and what should I track meanwhile?
  5. Which symptoms mean I should get help sooner?

During the appointment

  • Start with your summary: "Here is what the last three months looked like", then add your questions.
  • Be direct about the worst days. Do not edit the symptoms down to be polite.
  • Ask for the plan to be repeated or written down before you leave.
  • If you feel unheard, ask what you should do if the symptoms continue, and ask for that to be noted.

After the appointment

Write down what was said while you remember it, including any new medicine and the date you agreed to review it. Keep tracking: the same diary shows whether a change is helping, and it is the evidence for the next visit.

Frequently asked questions

What should I bring to an endometriosis appointment?

A one-page summary of your symptoms over the last two to three months, your pain scores, the dates of your periods, a list of medicines and treatments you have tried and how they worked, and your questions. A printed or on-phone symptom diary covers most of this.

What questions should I ask my doctor about endometriosis?

Ask what the next step is and why, what the options are and their trade-offs, what tests or scans could help, how long to try a treatment before reviewing it, and which symptoms mean you should get help sooner.

How long before an appointment should I start tracking symptoms?

Two to three months of tracking, covering at least two full cycles, is ideal. If your appointment is sooner, start today: even two weeks of daily pain scores gives your doctor more than memory alone.

What if my doctor dismisses my symptoms?

Stay calm, show your records, and ask what you should do if the symptoms continue. You can ask for your concerns to be written in your notes, ask for a referral, or seek a second opinion. Records make the pattern harder to overlook.
