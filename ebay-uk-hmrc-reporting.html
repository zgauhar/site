eBay, Vinted and HMRC: the 30 sales rule

Short answer: since 1 January 2024, eBay, Vinted, Depop and other platforms send HMRC the details of UK sellers who make 30 or more sales or receive more than €2,000 (about £1,700) on that platform in a calendar year. HMRC says a report does not automatically mean you owe tax, and you are unlikely to pay tax on personal items you sell from your home.

The rules come from The Platform Operators (Due Diligence and Reporting Requirements) Regulations 2023, the UK version of the OECD model rules that the EU calls DAC7. The money limit is set in euros (€2,000), which is about £1,700.

What is reported, and when

  • Your name, address, date of birth and National Insurance number or tax reference
  • The bank account used for payouts
  • The number of sales and the amounts you received

Platforms send the information for each calendar year to HMRC by 31 January of the next year (sales from 1 January to 31 December 2025 are reported by 31 January 2026) and must give you a copy of what they report.

Do you owe tax?

  • Personal possessions: HMRC says you probably do not have to pay Income Tax when you sell your own things, such as the contents of your loft or garage. Capital Gains Tax only comes in for an item (or set) sold for more than £6,000.
  • Trading: buying items to resell, or making things to sell, is likely to be trading. If your trading income is more than the £1,000 trading allowance in a tax year (6 April to 5 April), you need to tell HMRC.

Check HMRC’s guidance below, or ask HMRC or an accountant if you are not sure which applies to you.

Questions

Does eBay report my sales to HMRC?

Yes, if you make 30 or more sales or receive more than €2,000 (about £1,700) on eBay in a calendar year. Vinted, Depop and other platforms follow the same rules.

Do I have to pay tax on things I sell on eBay or Vinted?

Not usually for personal possessions. Buying things to resell is likely to be trading, and trading income above the £1,000 trading allowance has to be reported to HMRC.

Are the limits per platform?

Yes. Each platform counts your sales on its own.

Will I get a copy of what is reported?

Yes. Platforms must give you a copy of the information they send to HMRC.
