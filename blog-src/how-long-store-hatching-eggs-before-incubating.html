Set hatching eggs within 7 days of laying for the best hatch rate, and store them pointed end down at about 13 to 16 °C (55 to 60 °F) until then. Eggs can wait longer, up to about 10 days with little loss, but every extra day makes the hatch a bit weaker and a bit later. After two weeks the results are poor.

Why storage time matters

A fertile egg starts to develop only when it is warmed. Once laid and cooled, the embryo pauses, but it does not stay perfect forever. The white thins, the air cell grows as moisture escapes and the embryo slowly loses vigour. A stored egg therefore hatches later and less often than a fresh one, and weak chicks become more common.

Storage time at a glance

Days storedWhat to expect
0 to 7Best hatch rate, set whenever convenient
8 to 10Small drop in hatch rate, hatch may run a few hours late
11 to 14Clearly lower hatch rate, more weak chicks
Over 14Poor results, only worth it for rare eggs

These are typical guidelines. Species and breeds differ, and small eggs such as Coturnix quail lose moisture faster, so many keepers set quail eggs within about 7 days. Fresh, clean, well-fertilised eggs always do best.

How to store hatching eggs

  1. Collect often. Gather eggs at least once a day, and more in hot or freezing weather, so they never sit in a warm nest or freeze.
  2. Choose well. Keep only normal-shaped eggs with strong, undamaged shells. Leave out cracked, very small, very large or misshapen ones.
  3. Do not wash them. The natural coating on the shell keeps bacteria out. Dry-wipe a lightly soiled egg, and discard heavily soiled ones rather than scrubbing them.
  4. Store pointed end down in an egg carton or tray, with the blunt end (the air cell) up.
  5. Keep them cool, at about 13 to 16 °C (55 to 60 °F) and out of direct sun. A cellar or a cool utility room works. A fridge is too cold and too dry.
  6. Tilt them if they wait. From about day 4 to 7, tilt the carton to the opposite side once or twice a day, for example by propping one end of the tray on a book.
  7. Label the date laid in pencil on each egg or on the carton, so you can set the oldest first.

Warm the eggs before setting

Take the eggs out of storage 6 to 8 hours before you set them and leave them at room temperature. Going straight from a cool room into a 37.5 °C incubator can make condensation form on the shell, which can let bacteria in. Once warmed, load them with the pointed end down or on their sides, and start turning at least three times a day. See when to stop turning eggs for the end of the turning schedule.

Stored eggs hatch a little late

Do not be surprised if a batch of week-old eggs starts hatching a few hours after the due time. The hatch date calculator counts from the day you set the eggs, not the day they were laid, and the incubation chart lists the days for each species. If the eggs go in a few days after laying, count day 0 from the day they go into the incubator.

Plan the batch so storage never goes wrong

If you collect eggs for several days to fill a tray, the earliest eggs are the oldest by the time you set them. Record each laying date and set the batch once you have enough, ideally within the 7-day window.
