The best power nap is 10 to 20 minutes of sleep, so set your nap timer for 20 to 25 minutes to allow time to drop off. That keeps you in light sleep and you wake up alert instead of groggy. If you have an hour and a half, a full sleep cycle of about 90 minutes is the other length that works. Naps between 30 and 60 minutes are the ones that leave you feeling worse.

The timer setting adds a few minutes for falling asleep. If you usually drop off in two minutes, shorten it; if you take ten, lengthen it.

Why 20 minutes works and 45 minutes does not

Sleep moves in cycles of roughly 90 minutes. In the first 20 minutes or so you are in light sleep, which is easy to wake from. After that you slide into deep, slow-wave sleep. Woken from deep sleep you get sleep inertia: heavy limbs, a foggy head and worse reaction times than before the nap, for up to half an hour. A short timer stops the nap before that slide.

A NASA study of long-haul pilots in the 1990s is the source of the "NASA nap": a planned nap averaging 26 minutes in the cockpit seat improved performance by 34% and physiological alertness by 54% compared with pilots who did not nap.

How to set up a power nap in four steps

  1. Pick the time. Early afternoon, roughly 1 to 3 pm, fits the natural afternoon dip. Napping after about 4 pm can make it harder to fall asleep at night.
  2. Set the countdown before you lie down. 20 to 25 minutes for a power nap. Name the timer "Nap" so the alarm tells you what it is.
  3. Make it easy to drop off. Dark, quiet, slightly cool. An eye mask helps. Resting with your eyes closed still helps if you do not fall asleep.
  4. Get up when it rings. Snoozing turns a 20-minute nap into a 40-minute one, which is the groggy kind. Stand up, get light and drink some water.

The coffee nap

Caffeine takes about 20 minutes to reach full effect. Drink a coffee, then nap straight away for 15 to 20 minutes, and the caffeine starts working just as the alarm goes. Driving studies have found the combination reduces sleepiness more than either on its own. Skip it later in the day if caffeine keeps you awake at night.

Common nap mistakes

  • No timer. "Just a few minutes" without an alarm is how a 20-minute nap turns into two hours.
  • Napping too late. A long or late nap borrows from night sleep.
  • Using naps instead of sleep. A nap tops up; it does not replace seven to nine hours. If you need a nap every day despite sleeping well, mention it to a doctor.
  • Driving straight after. Give yourself 10 to 15 minutes to shake off any grogginess first.
