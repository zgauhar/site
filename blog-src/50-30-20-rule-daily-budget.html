The 50/30/20 rule splits your after-tax income into 50% needs, 30% wants and 20% savings and debt repayment. It is simple, but it stops at the monthly level, so on any given day you still do not know whether you can afford lunch out. The fix is to divide the wants bucket by the days in your pay period. Below you will find a worked example, what to do when your needs cost too much and how to track the daily number.

What does 50/30/20 mean?

BucketShare of net incomeWhat goes in it
Needs50%Rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, transport, insurance, minimum debt payments
Wants30%Eating out, hobbies, subscriptions, travel, shopping
Savings and debt20%Emergency fund, investing, extra debt repayment

The rule was popularised by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi in the book All Your Worth. It uses net income, the money that reaches your account after tax. It is a guideline, not a law.

Worked example: 2,400 a month

  1. Needs: 1,200. Rent, bills, groceries and transport must fit here.
  2. Savings: 480. Move it on payday, before you can spend it.
  3. Wants: 720. This is the money you can spend freely.
  4. Daily wants limit: 24. 720 divided by 30 days.

If it is day 10 and you have spent 300 of the wants bucket, you have 420 left for 20 days, so your limit is now 21 a day. This is the same idea as in our guide on how much you can spend per day, with the buckets as the starting point.

When needs are more than 50%

  • Change the ratio. 60/20/20 or 60/25/15 are common in high-rent cities.
  • Cut wants first. Needs are fixed in the short term, so the wants bucket absorbs the difference.
  • Keep savings above zero. Even 5 to 10% builds a buffer and the habit.

Where the rule falls short

It does not handle uneven pay, weekly or fortnightly paydays or big one-off costs. If you are paid every week or twice a month, read how to budget with weekly, biweekly or twice-monthly pay, and apply the percentages per pay period instead of per month.

Track the daily number without a bank login

Daily Budget is a budget app that shows a daily allowance from your income, bills and savings. It carries unspent money forward, follows weekly, every-2-weeks, twice-a-month or monthly pay cycles and explains every step of today's number. It never asks for a bank login and has no ads, and your budget data stays on your phone. Enter your needs and savings as fixed amounts and the daily allowance is your wants limit.
