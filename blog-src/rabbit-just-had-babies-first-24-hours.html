When a doe has just kindled, check the nest the same day: count the kits, remove any dead ones, warm any that are cold, and make sure the nest is lined with fur. After that, leave them be. A mother rabbit feeds her kits only once or twice a day, usually at night, so a doe that ignores the nest is normal. What matters is whether the kits look fed.

The first-day checklist

  1. Give the doe a treat first. Check the nest while she is out of it or busy eating, so she stays calm.
  2. Count the kits. Note how many were born alive and how many dead. This is the number that tells you, over several litters, which does to keep.
  3. Remove dead kits and afterbirth. Leaving them in the nest attracts flies and spreads bacteria.
  4. Look for kits outside the nest. Kits born on the wire or dragged out while nursing chill fast. Warm them before putting them back (see below).
  5. Check the fur. The kits should be in a hollow under a layer of fur. If she pulled little or none, add fur she has shed or clean, dry nesting material, and cover them.
  6. Keep the nest dry. Replace wet bedding around the kits without disturbing the fur pile more than needed.
  7. Look after the doe. Plenty of fresh water and hay, and her usual feed. A nursing doe eats and drinks more over the coming weeks.

Is the mother feeding her babies?

Does nurse for only a few minutes, once or twice in 24 hours, usually at night or dawn. That is how rabbits are made, not neglect, so judge the kits rather than the doe:

Fed kitsHungry kits
Round, full bellies; you may see milk through the skinThin, flat bellies
Smooth, filled-out skinWrinkled, loose skin
Sleep quietly in a pileRestless, crawling around, may cry

If the kits look hungry after a day, especially from a first-time doe, ask your vet or an experienced breeder. Some breeders hold the doe over the nest once or twice a day so the kits can nurse, and large litters are sometimes split with another doe that kindled within a few days. Hand-rearing newborn kits is hard and has low success, so it is a last resort, done with a vet's advice.

Warming a cold kit

A kit found away from the nest can feel cold and look lifeless and still recover. Warm it slowly: in your hands against your body, or near a warm (not hot) water bottle wrapped in a towel. Once it moves and feels warm, tuck it back into the fur with its littermates. Never use direct heat such as a hair dryer or heat lamp close up, which can burn or overheat it.

When to call the vet

  • The doe keeps straining or seems in pain after kindling: a kit may be stuck.
  • Heavy bleeding, or she stops eating or drinking.
  • Hot, hard, red or swollen teats over the next days.
  • The kits still look hungry after 24 to 48 hours.

What comes next

Kits open their eyes at about 10 to 12 days, leave the nest at about 3 weeks and are weaned at 4 to 8 weeks. Weigh the whole litter on day 21: that weight shows how well the doe milks.
