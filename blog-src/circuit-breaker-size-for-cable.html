To choose a breaker for a cable, follow Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz: the breaker rating In must be at least the load current Ib and no higher than the cable's current-carrying capacity Iz. The breaker protects the cable, so a breaker bigger than the cable can carry lets the cable overheat before anything trips. This is the core overload rule in IEC 60364, and it is easy to apply once you have the corrected cable rating.

What Ib, In and Iz mean

  • Ib: the design current of the circuit, the most the load will draw in normal use.
  • In: the rated current of the breaker or fuse, for example 16, 20 or 25 A.
  • Iz: the current the cable can carry continuously, after correction for ambient temperature, grouping and installation method.

If a load draws 18 A, any breaker from 20 A up can carry it, but only if Iz is at least that breaker rating too. The best choice is the smallest standard breaker above Ib that the cable also covers.

The "common maximum" is lower than Iz on purpose: it leaves a margin for derating, and breakers come in fixed steps. Your national wiring rules may set different limits, so check them for the final design.

Worked example: an electric shower

An 8.5 kW shower on 230 V draws about 37 A, so Ib = 37 A.

  1. The next standard breaker above 37 A is 40 A, so In = 40 A.
  2. 6 mm² has Iz = 46 A in method C. That is at least 40 A, so it passes. 4 mm² (36 A) does not.
  3. If the cable is buried in warm insulation, apply a correction factor. At 0.8, Iz drops from 46 A to 36.8 A and the 6 mm² no longer covers a 40 A breaker. You would move up to 10 mm².

The check is simple, but the derating step is where many installs go wrong. The related guides on voltage drop on long cable runs show the next check you need.

Why a bigger breaker is dangerous

A breaker rated above Iz can let a sustained overload run through the cable. The cable heats beyond its insulation limit, 70 °C for PVC, with no trip. The fix is never "a bigger breaker": it is a bigger cable, or a lower-rated breaker, or a smaller load.

Checklist before you pick a breaker

  • Work out the design current Ib from power and voltage.
  • Choose the smallest standard In at or above Ib.
  • Take Iz from the table for your cable, insulation and installation method.
  • Apply correction factors for temperature and grouping to get the corrected Iz.
  • Check In ≤ corrected Iz. If not, step the cable up a size.
  • Check voltage drop and, where required, fault-loop impedance and trip time.
